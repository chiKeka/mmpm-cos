# Edmonton Valley Line SE — DP3: Signalling integration approach (late 2019)

## Context at decision moment

It is late 2019. The Valley Line Southeast is approximately 12 months from its target December 2020 opening. Construction is substantially complete for most civil and systems scopes. Integrated testing of the signalling, vehicle-control, and operational systems has commenced. The TransEd consortium (Bechtel, EllisDon, Bombardier, Fengate) is the DBFOM concessionaire; Bombardier Transportation supplies the vehicles and is integration-responsible on the vehicle-signalling interface. The line's signalling configuration involves low-floor urban LRT operation at-grade through urban corridors and in dedicated right-of-way, with the elevated river-valley crossing and station platforms.

Integrated testing has surfaced issues: intermittent signalling behaviour in specific track-circuit configurations; vehicle-software interaction problems at crossings; interfaces between operational and safety systems behaving unexpectedly under certain conditions. TransEd has proposed a remediation plan with an updated integration testing schedule. The proposed path shows a continued plan for opening in 2020.

The DBFOM contract provides the City of Edmonton with deductions for late opening, specific performance-assurance mechanisms, and rights to require additional testing evidence. The City's concession-management team is weighing whether to accept TransEd's proposed remediation path, require additional evidence before accepting, or begin engaging commercial-default mechanisms.

## The decision

The City Administration and Council must decide whether to accept TransEd's proposed signalling remediation plan and opening-date maintenance, require additional integrated-testing evidence before acceptance, invoke enhanced performance-monitoring provisions under the concession, or begin to formally document potential default scenarios.

## What is known

- Integrated testing has surfaced specific issues.
- TransEd has proposed a remediation path.
- The DBFOM contract contains performance-assurance and deductions provisions.
- Bombardier's broader rolling-stock and signalling delivery performance across Canadian programmes has been mixed.
- The December 2020 opening date has been publicly reinforced by the City and TransEd.
- Canadian P3 transit reference class shows late-programme integration as a canonical failure mode.
- ETS and City communications operations are scheduled for December 2020 opening.

## What is assumed

- factual_assumption: TransEd's remediation plan is technically sound and adequately scoped.
- behavioural_assumption: TransEd's reporting on remediation progress is accurate.
- evidence_assumption: Current testing evidence is representative of full-scope operational performance.
- temporal_assumption: Remaining integration-testing scope can be completed by the opening date.
- structural_assumption: The concession's performance-assurance mechanisms are adequate to manage the emerging situation.

## What is contested

- Adequacy of current testing evidence.
- Whether the remediation plan addresses root cause or symptom.
- Whether the opening date can be maintained with operational-safety integrity.
- Whether commercial engagement is premature or overdue.
- Severity of integration issues relative to comparable programmes.

## What is unknown

- True extent of signalling-integration defects under all operational scenarios.
- Consortium financial-bandwidth for extended remediation.
- Downstream rework-risk if opening is maintained and defects surface post-opening.

## The question posed to Council

> You are an advisor to the City of Edmonton's concession management team. The City is deciding how to respond to TransEd's proposed signalling-remediation plan and continued commitment to a December 2020 opening. What is your analysis? What would you recommend, with what confidence, and why?
