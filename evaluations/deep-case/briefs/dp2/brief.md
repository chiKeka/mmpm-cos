# Edmonton Valley Line SE — DP2: Alignment and technology decisions (2014–2015)

## Context at decision moment

The Valley Line Southeast P3 DBFOM delivery approach has been committed by City Council (see DP1). Procurement has been initiated. During 2014 and 2015, several interlocking scope decisions require resolution before Request for Proposals issuance: rolling-stock technology choice (low-floor urban LRT vs high-floor), alignment specifics through contested urban corridors (in particular the 75 Street / 83 Street corridor and the Whyte Avenue area interface), grade-separation choices (at-grade vs elevated segments), and the river-valley crossing design (bridge type and alignment).

The low-floor urban LRT choice is advanced by City staff and consultants as aligned with international urban-tram best practice and the urban-integration objectives of the line, distinct from Edmonton's existing high-floor Capital and Metro lines. Community associations in the affected corridors have raised concerns about at-grade running, traffic interface, and disruption. Edmonton Transit Service has expressed operational-interface concerns about a parallel low-floor system.

## The decision

City Council, on staff and consultant advice, must confirm the technology, alignment, and grade-separation scope that will go into the RFP. These scope decisions lock-in the bidders' design basis for a 30-year concession. Deferral or re-opening post-RFP is contractually expensive.

## What is known

- Low-floor urban LRT experience exists internationally but Canadian cold-climate reference class is limited.
- At-grade running at the proposed speeds in the specified corridors has specific traffic-interface, pedestrian-safety, and operational implications.
- Existing Edmonton LRT network operates high-floor vehicles with different platform heights.
- Community associations in 75 Street / 83 Street / Whyte Avenue corridors have raised objections.
- The 30-year DBFOM concession will lock in rolling-stock choice, signalling, and operational parameters.
- PPP Canada funding is contingent on timely achievement of financial close.
- Edmonton Transit Service operational interfaces (driver training, maintenance, control centre) are separate but adjacent.

## What is assumed

- factual_assumption: Low-floor urban LRT technology is adequately mature for Edmonton winter operation.
- structural_assumption: Two LRT systems (high-floor Capital/Metro + low-floor Valley Line) can co-exist operationally without disruption.
- behavioural_assumption: Affected communities will accept at-grade running with standard mitigation.
- temporal_assumption: Alignment decisions can be finalised within RFP timeline.
- evidence_assumption: Consultant advice on technology and alignment reflects robust analysis.

## What is contested

- Low-floor vs high-floor technology choice and operational implications.
- At-grade running in urban corridors vs elevation.
- River-valley crossing design.
- Operational interface with existing ETS network.
- Community engagement adequacy.

## What is unknown

- Behaviour of cumulative technical novelty (low-floor + at-grade urban + novel signalling + 30-year concession).
- Long-term operational reliability of the chosen configuration.
- Evolution of community posture during construction.

## The question posed to Council

> You are an advisor to Edmonton City Council. Council is deciding on the technology, alignment, and grade-separation scope to go into the Valley Line Southeast P3 RFP. What is your analysis? What would you recommend, with what confidence, and why?
