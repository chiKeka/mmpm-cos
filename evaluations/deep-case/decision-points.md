# Edmonton Valley Line Southeast LRT — Deep Case Study

**Status**: DRAFT v0 — decision points identified, briefs constructed (`briefs/dp1-dp4/brief.md`). Awaiting Council runs.

## Purpose

A narrative-format deep case study that runs `/council` on four critical decision points across the lifecycle of the Valley Line Southeast LRT, then compares Council outputs against what MMPM literature would predict should have been surfaced.

This workstream is:
- A complement to the benchmark study, not a substitute.
- Denser on a single case, where benchmark is shallower across eight.
- Locally grounded — the submitter has practitioner context from the Edmonton environment.
- Non-quantitative — a 4-6 page narrative writeup, not a scored rubric.

## Case overview

The Valley Line Southeast (VLSE) is a 13 km urban low-floor LRT line in Edmonton, delivered under a P3 DBFOM contract awarded to TransEd Partners in 2016. Originally scheduled to open in December 2020, it opened in November 2022 with ongoing quality issues around the signalling system and vehicle integration.

## Proposed decision points (4)

### DP1 — Delivery model commitment (2013)

**What is being decided**: Whether to deliver the Valley Line as a P3 DBFOM, a traditional public build, or an alternative procurement model. Committed to P3 structure in 2013.

**MMPM-relevant dynamics to test Council on**:
- Risk-transfer evaluation (did the VFM fairly price what was being transferred?)
- Governance fragmentation implications of 30-year DBFOM
- Path dependence (once P3 signed, how does it shape future decisions?)
- Long-horizon contracting with a consortium structure

### DP2 — Alignment and grade-separation decisions (2014-2015)

**What is being decided**: Low-floor vs high-floor technology, at-grade vs elevated sections through urban corridors.

**MMPM-relevant dynamics**:
- Stakeholder fragmentation (neighbourhoods, business associations, city council, TransEd)
- Technology-maturity risk for low-floor urban LRT in Canadian winter
- Interface risks between the new technology and legacy Capital/Metro line operations
- Optimism bias on operational performance assumptions

### DP3 — Signalling system integration approach (2018-2019)

**What is being decided**: How to resolve emerging signalling and interface problems as integration testing progresses. Sub-decisions around scope, sequencing, penalty regime engagement.

**MMPM-relevant dynamics**:
- Escalation of commitment on a chosen technical path
- Assurance capture (is the independent certifier catching issues?)
- Tight coupling between signalling, vehicle, and track systems
- Commercial incentive misalignment in a penalty-based contract

### DP4 — Opening commitment and deferral decisions (2020-2022)

**What is being decided**: Whether to accept, defer, or contest TransEd's successive readiness declarations and opening-date proposals.

**MMPM-relevant dynamics**:
- Governance under commercial distress
- Legitimacy exposure (riders, elected officials, Edmonton's reputation as a transit city)
- Risk-transfer reality test (did risks return to the city under the payment regime?)
- Sunk-cost reasoning at the tail of a programme

## Method

For each decision point:

1. Construct a decision brief per `benchmark/brief-template.md`, sourced from:
   - City of Edmonton council documents (public)
   - Edmonton Transit Service documents
   - Alberta Auditor General reports where applicable
   - Journalism (Edmonton Journal, CBC Edmonton contemporaneous reporting)
   - TransEd public statements at the time
   - Post-operation reviews
2. Run `/council` on the brief.
3. Separately, compile "literature-expected" analysis — what Flyvbjerg on megaproject political economy, Drummond on escalation of commitment, Perrow on tight coupling, Ansar et al. on infrastructure fat tails, and Scott / Jacobs on urban legitimacy would predict should have been surfaced.
4. Narrative comparison: where does Council converge with literature prediction, where does it diverge, and is divergence a system weakness or a genuine insight?

## Output

`evaluations/deep-case/findings.md` — 4-6 page narrative writeup with:
- Case summary (one page)
- Per-DP: brief, Council output summary, literature comparison table (one page per DP)
- Synthesis: where does Council add signal on this case, where does it miss, and what does it suggest about the system's limits? (one page)
- Limitations specific to this case (single city, single case, practitioner-familiar scorer) (half page)

## What this workstream does not claim

- Not a claim about generalizability from a single case.
- Not an indictment of any specific actor in the programme.
- Not a retrospective scorecard — the question is whether Council's analysis *at the decision moment* would have been useful, not whether it predicts outcomes.
