# W4 — Edmonton Valley Line Southeast: Deep Case Findings

**Status**: COMPLETE (first pass) — all four DP Council runs done and compared against `literature-expected.md` 2026-04-20. Synthesis populated. No further runs planned; revisions would reflect user feedback.

Target length: 4-6 pages. Current length ~5 pages.

## 1. Case summary

The Valley Line Southeast (VLSE) is a 13 km urban low-floor LRT line in Edmonton, delivered under a P3 DBFOM contract awarded to TransEd Partners (Bechtel, EllisDon, Bombardier, Fengate) in 2016. Originally scheduled to open December 2020, it opened 4 November 2022 — approximately 23 months late. Delay was driven principally by signalling-integration and vehicle-software issues. Capital cost remained broadly within the reported envelope though penalty mechanisms and contractual disputes affected the commercial position. Post-opening operational reliability has been mixed.

The case is selected for W4 because it (i) is documented in the public record, (ii) is locally relevant to the scorer (practitioner context per `practitioner-context/`), (iii) provides a Canadian P3 transit reference at small-enough scale for deep examination, and (iv) the outcome (delivered late with integration problems) makes it a genuine test case: not a spectacular failure and not a clean success — a mixed-outcome programme where contemporaneous analysis must read the signals well to add value.

Four decision points span the lifecycle:
- **DP1 — 2013**: Delivery-model commitment (P3 DBFOM vs PSC). *Reuses W1 Edmonton Council output.*
- **DP2 — 2014–2015**: Alignment, low-floor technology, grade-separation, RFP scope.
- **DP3 — late 2019**: Signalling-integration remediation plan acceptance; December 2020 opening.
- **DP4 — late 2021**: Mid-2022 opening declaration after successive missed dates; Bombardier-Alstom transition.

## 6. Synthesis

Across all four decision points, Council surfaces **30 of 30 literature-expected dimensions** (8 + 7 + 7 + 8), with explicit MMPM-citation where the literature identifies one (Flyvbjerg, Siemiatycki, Morris, Scott, Perrow, Merrow, Beetham, Drummond). Council additionally surfaces **8 dimensions** not in the literature-expected list (2 per DP on average), ranging from strategic-misrepresentation framings to methodological reframes.

### Where Council adds reliable signal

(i) **Dimensions with dedicated MMPM-COS routing destinations surface cleanly**. Stakeholder stratigraphy → Impact + Governance agents. Cumulative novelty → Risk + Emergence. Risk-transfer realism → Commercial. Assurance capture → Governance. Temporal clock mismatch → Temporal Dynamics. Public-value framings → Public Value & Ethics. The routing rules in `configs/routing/routing-rules.md` map the decision dimensions to agents; when the rules engage, the surfacing follows.

(ii) **Cross-DP consistency**. The same structural framings (risk-transfer realism, 30-year horizon mismatch, reference-class fragility) recur across DP1 / DP3 / DP4 appropriately — they are features of the programme, not of the specific moment.

(iii) **Methodological reframes**. Each DP produces at least one reframe not in the brief (sequenced decisions at DP3; framework-re-examination at DP4; option-space expansion at DP1; cumulative-novelty framing at DP2). These are the strongest candidate contributions because they change what the decision-maker is being asked to decide.

### Where Council misses or under-weights

(i) **Path-dependence labelling at DP1**. Council surfaces the mechanism (30-year lock-in) without always applying the specific "path dependence" label. Literature framework-users who want the concept named explicitly would mark this 1.5/2 rather than 2/2.

(ii) **Technical-systems analysis at DP3**. Council appropriately reframes to "independent verification before evaluation" because it cannot judge the technical state from the brief. A reviewer who wanted Council to *assess* the remediation plan's technical merit would find the system limited. This is a scoping truthfulness, not a failure.

(iii) **Quantitative calibration**. Council's reference-class base rates (e.g., "~45% cost overrun") come from Flyvbjerg's global dataset. Canadian-specific calibration (if it differs) is not applied. A reviewer with Canadian P3 transit quantitative data could critique the specific numbers.

### Patterns suggesting system limits

Council performs strongly on decision dimensions that map to its routing architecture (governance, risk, commercial, impact, public value, temporal, emergence). Decisions that turn on dimensions outside these destinations — deep technical-systems analysis, detailed legal-contract interpretation, specific actuarial computation — would require specialist inputs Council does not provide. The Council method is well-matched to governance-level programme decisions and poorly-matched to technical or legal detail; this is an architectural feature, not a shortcoming.

### Material observation on outcome

The Valley Line Southeast eventually opened ~23 months late with integration and reliability issues. DP2 contemporaneous Council analysis surfaced cumulative-novelty risk; DP3 contemporaneous Council analysis surfaced assurance-capture risk and reference-class integration-delay base rate; DP4 contemporaneous Council analysis weighted TransEd's prediction track record heavily. The analyses do not predict specific fault modes (signalling-X defect at track-circuit-Y) — they predict the *pattern* (cumulative-novelty integration issues producing schedule slip). The pattern materialised. This is a positive case for the method's ability to read decision-moment signals, *discounted* by the designer-as-scorer confound: the operator producing the Council outputs has outcome knowledge and practitioner context, and cannot fully bracket them.

## 7. Limitations

- **Single case, single city, single delivery model**. The 30-of-30 surfacing result is descriptive of VLSE only.
- **Scorer is a practitioner with local context**. This enriches analysis but also introduces perspective. Scorer knowledge of the actual VLSE outcome (opened November 2022 with signalling and reliability issues) cannot be fully bracketed during literature-comparison scoring. The material hindsight-discipline note in the W1 Edmonton `ground-truth.md` applies to W4 too.
- **Designer-as-scorer information flow on W4**: the literature-expected.md file and all four Council outputs were authored by the same operator in the same session. The literature-expected list was authored before the Council outputs, but the operator has it in context while producing the Council outputs. Council is being scored against a target authored by its operator. The 30/30 surfacing rate should be discounted accordingly. Mitigation: literature citations (Flyvbjerg, Siemiatycki, Morris, Scott, Perrow, Merrow, Beetham, Drummond) are external to MMPM-COS; a reviewer using a different MMPM-literature interpretation would likely produce a similar list, but the magnitude of the 30/30 result is operator-aligned and partly confirms what the operator is looking for.
- **Post-hoc DP selection**. The four decision points were chosen to span the lifecycle and to test Council at different phases. A different selection might yield different conclusions.
- **Literature-expected baseline is interpretation, not independently constructed**. A reader could disagree with specific entries.
- **DP2–DP4 outputs are single-run** per condition — per `benchmark/prompts/council.md` reproducibility note, Council was run once per DP; multiple runs may produce variation in surfacing coverage.
- **Structural reference-class calibration** in Council comes from global megaproject literature. Canadian P3 transit-specific base rates may differ from global rail-megaproject averages; the Council analysis does not always distinguish.
- **Mixed-outcome case difficulty**. VLSE is a mixed outcome (delivered, functional, late, with reliability issues). The methodology's discriminative power is harder to assess than on clean failures or clean successes. A reader could argue that Council's surfacing would have been less comprehensive on a case where the structural framings were wrong — the VLSE case does not test that.
