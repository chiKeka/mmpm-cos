## 1. Case summary

The Valley Line Southeast (VLSE) is a 13 km urban low-floor LRT line in Edmonton, delivered under a P3 DBFOM contract awarded to TransEd Partners in 2016. Originally scheduled to open December 2020, it opened November 2022 with ongoing quality issues around the signalling system and vehicle integration. The case is selected for W4 because it (i) is documented in the public record, (ii) is locally relevant to the scorer (practitioner context per `practitioner-context/`), and (iii) provides a Canadian P3 transit reference at small enough scale to permit deep examination across four decision points.

## 3. Decision Point 2 — Alignment and technology (2014-2015)

**Brief summary** ([briefs/dp2/brief.md](briefs/dp2/brief.md)): with the P3 DBFOM model already committed (DP1), City Council must confirm interlocking pre-RFP scope decisions: low-floor urban LRT vs high-floor; at-grade vs elevated through 75 Street / 83 Street and Whyte Avenue corridors; river-valley crossing; alignment specifics. The decisions lock in the bidders' design basis for a 30-year concession.

**Council output** ([council-runs/dp2.md](council-runs/dp2.md)): Council recommends ratify-with-three-strengthening-conditions: (i) cumulative-novelty risk-management plan via joint-stress sensitivity analysis on the four scope dimensions; (ii) stakeholder-stratigraphy engagement reaching second-layer affected populations beyond formal consultation; (iii) 30-year concession governance plan with periodic 5-year structured reviews. Reframes the problem from independent-engineering-choices to **cumulative novelty in a configuration outside any well-populated reference class** (Merrow). Surfaces stakeholder stratigraphy (Scott, Morris) explicitly. Names socio-material effects on Whyte Avenue character, river valley, corridor businesses. Identifies path-dependence of pre-RFP scope-fix. Adversarial review preserves Contrarian dissent on novelty premium magnitude.

DP2 surfacing rate: **7 of 7 literature-predicted dimensions** raised by Council. Each dimension is explicit, attributed to a specific section, and in most cases attributed to specific MMPM citation (Merrow, Perrow, Scott, Morris). Council also adds two dimensions not in the literature-expected list:

- **Bidder thinness for the configuration** under DBFOM with specific technical choices (§4.2).
- **Public-value framing of low-floor-vs-high-floor itself** as a values commitment about what the line is for, not just a technical choice (§6.3 (iii)).

These additional dimensions are reasonable extensions; whether they belong in the literature-expected list is a judgment call.

**Material observation on this DP**: the configuration that City Council ratified in 2014–2015 produced a programme that opened ~2 years late with documented signalling-integration issues and ongoing vehicle-reliability concerns. With hindsight, the cumulative-novelty premium was real. Council's contemporaneous-stripped analysis surfaces it without invoking the outcome. This is a positive case for the Council method on the W4 deep-case test.

## 7. Limitations

- Single case, single city, single delivery model.
- Scorer is a practitioner with local context — this enriches analysis but also introduces perspective. Specifically: scorer knowledge of the actual VLSE outcome (opened ~2 years late with quality issues) cannot be fully bracketed during literature-comparison scoring.
- Post-hoc decision-point selection (the four chosen are ones where Council's performance is interesting to examine; a different selection might yield different conclusions).
- The "literature prediction" baseline is itself the scorer's interpretation of MMPM literature — it is not independently constructed. A reader could disagree with specific entries.
- DP2 Council run was produced after the scorer had read the literature-expected.md file, which is a separate designer-as-scorer information-flow concern from the W1 D4 issue documented in `evaluations/benchmark/scores/scored-rubric.csv` header. The literature-expected dimensions were authored by the same operator that produced the Council output, in the same session — Council is being scored against a target authored by its operator. Mitigation: literature citations (Merrow, Scott, Perrow) are external to MMPM-COS; a reviewer using a different MMPM-literature interpretation would likely produce a similar list. But the surfacing-rate result (7/7) should be discounted accordingly.
