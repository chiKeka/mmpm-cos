# Literature-Expected Analysis — What MMPM Scholarship Would Predict Should Be Surfaced

**Status**: DRAFT v0 — to be used as baseline for the W4 deep-case Council comparison.

This document records what MMPM literature would predict a careful analyst should surface at each Valley Line Southeast decision point. It is the baseline against which Council outputs are compared in W4, independent of the W1 rubric.

## Sources of literature-expected predictions

- Flyvbjerg (reference class forecasting, optimism bias, strategic misrepresentation, megaproject political economy)
- Drummond (escalation of commitment in megaprojects)
- Perrow (normal accidents, tight coupling in complex systems)
- Ansar et al. (fat-tail infrastructure costs)
- Merrow / IPA (benchmarking literature for megaproject delivery)
- Siemiatycki, Vining & Boardman, Murray (Canadian P3 literature)
- Scott, Jacobs (urban legitimacy and local knowledge)
- Beetham (legitimacy theory applied to governance)
- Morris, Miller & Lessard (major programme management doctrine)

## DP1 — Delivery model commitment (2013)

Literature would predict that a careful analyst should surface:

- **Optimism bias in the VfM analysis** — Flyvbjerg on systematic understatement of capital cost and schedule; reference-class critique.
- **Risk-transfer realism under distress** — Siemiatycki, Vining & Boardman on the difference between formal and effective risk transfer in Canadian P3s.
- **Canadian P3 transit reference class** — Canada Line, Confederation Line, Eglinton Crosstown schedule and cost performance.
- **30-year concession governance horizon mismatches political-continuity horizon** — Morris on governance-stakeholder arrangements.
- **Low-floor urban LRT novelty in Canadian cold climate** — limited reference class signal.
- **Operational interface with existing ETS high-floor network** creates governance fragmentation.
- **Path dependence** — the 2013 commitment forecloses alternatives through the 30-year horizon.
- **Legitimacy exposure** of the City despite contractual risk allocation — Beetham.

## DP2 — Alignment and technology (2014-2015)

Literature would predict:

- **Cumulative technical novelty** — Merrow's point that novelty in multiple dimensions compounds rather than adds.
- **Stakeholder stratigraphy** — affected-neighbourhood layers below formal engagement — Scott, Morris.
- **Socio-material effects** of at-grade urban running on affected communities — not priced in conventional BCR.
- **Lock-in** of 30-year concession to technology choices made pre-RFP — path dependence.
- **Optimism bias in integration-risk assessment** — Perrow's normal accidents applied to urban LRT integration.
- **Reference-class comparators for at-grade low-floor in cold climate** are limited.
- **Political time-horizon mismatch** — alignment decisions will outlive the current Council.

## DP3 — Signalling integration (late 2019)

Literature would predict:

- **Assurance capture** — NAO / PAC literature on late-programme on-track reporting.
- **Tight coupling** in late-programme signalling integration — Perrow.
- **Optimism bias in late-programme schedule assessments** — Flyvbjerg / Kahneman planning fallacy.
- **Commercial incentives shape reporting** — Siemiatycki on concessionaire reporting under penalty regimes.
- **Escalation of commitment** to the December 2020 opening date given public communications — Drummond.
- **Canadian P3 reference class on late-programme integration failures** — Confederation Line experience documented.
- **Principal-agent issues between sponsor and concessionaire during distress**.

## DP4 — Opening commitment under successive deferrals (late 2021)

Literature would predict:

- **Escalation of commitment** to continued deferral acceptance — Drummond.
- **Legitimacy erosion** — Beetham applied to programmes; on-time-on-budget frame misses legitimacy cost.
- **Risk-transfer realism** — formal contractual risk allocation vs effective sponsor exposure under distress.
- **Path dependence in the concession framework** — by this point, alternatives are constrained.
- **Cost asymmetry** between premature opening (operational-reliability risk) and continued deferral (legitimacy risk).
- **Temporal mismatch** — new Council term, incoming critical members, long-horizon operational governance.
- **Public value considerations** beyond contractual performance metrics — Public Value and Ethics.
- **Counterparty-change risk** — Bombardier to Alstom acquisition affecting delivery capability.
