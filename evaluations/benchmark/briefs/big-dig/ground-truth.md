# Big Dig — Ground Truth

## Outcome summary

The CA/T programme received federal approval and proceeded. Construction began in 1991 after an extended pre-construction period of design development, right-of-way acquisition, and utility relocation. Final cost at completion exceeded approximately $14.6 billion in nominal dollars, with total cost including financing/interest exceeding $22 billion — a cost growth of roughly 5–8× the 1985 estimate depending on accounting basis. Construction extended to approximately 2007 (the project is considered substantially complete though various punch-list items extended beyond). Quality failures included leaks in the tunnel system and the July 2006 fatal ceiling-panel collapse in the Ted Williams Tunnel connector. Multiple state investigations and federal oversight actions followed. The elevated Central Artery was removed and replaced with the Rose Kennedy Greenway park system. Traffic and urban-quality benefits are substantial. The cost overrun and quality failures are, together with the eventual delivered outcome, the defining features of the programme's reputation.

## Risk classes that materialised

- **Optimism bias in capital cost** — cost grew by a factor of roughly 5–8×.
- **Tight coupling in urban subsurface construction** — utility relocation, archaeology, contaminated soils, MBTA interface, and structural design interacted in unpredictable ways.
- **Governance fragmentation and accountability diffusion** — the MDPW / MHD / MTA / FHWA / B/PB structure produced accountability diffusion.
- **Commercial misalignment** — consultant, contractor, and management-of-works structures produced quality failures with delayed consequences.
- **Novel-technology / novel-construction integration** — urban construction above active utilities at this scale lacked direct reference class.
- **Escalation of commitment** — cost increases were accommodated incrementally; programme termination was never realistic after substantial sunk cost.
- **Quality / legitimacy risk materialising years after completion commitment** — the 2006 ceiling collapse illustrates that safety/quality risk periods extend beyond construction.
- **Political time-horizon mismatch** — funding and scope decisions outlasted multiple federal and state administrations.

## Contemporaneous markers for D2 scoring

Signals identifiable from material available in 1985:

1. **Urban construction of this scale above active utilities and transit infrastructure had limited domestic reference class** — the novelty was visible.
2. **Standard infrastructure optimism-bias research and historical cost-growth patterns on complex federal projects were publishable and known** in 1985.
3. **Joint-venture management consultant structure concentrated accountability diffusion risk** — a governance-design choice visible at decision moment.
4. **Utility relocation complexity in downtown Boston** was documented in the EIS record.
5. **Multi-agency coordination requirement (FHWA, MDPW, MBTA, utility owners, City of Boston)** was visible.
6. **Immersed-tube tunnelling method in an active harbour had specific construction-risk signatures** knowable at the time.
7. **A 10-year construction horizon across successive federal administrations exposed the programme to political-funding risk**.
8. **Environmental mitigation and displacement mitigation costs were contested in the EIS record** — often a marker of cost growth to follow.
