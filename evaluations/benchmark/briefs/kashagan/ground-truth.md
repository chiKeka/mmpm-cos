# Kashagan — Ground Truth

**SEALED — do not open during brief construction or output reading.**

## Outcome summary

Phase 1 FID was approved. Construction proceeded but cost and schedule performance deteriorated substantially. Phase 1 capital cost escalated from the ~$10bn estimate to approximately $50–57bn by first-oil achievement. First oil was repeatedly delayed from the 2005 target to 2013. Shortly after first oil in September 2013, sulphur-induced cracking was discovered in sour-service gas pipelines, forcing the field to be shut in. After pipeline remediation, first sustained production recommenced in 2016 — more than a decade after the original target. Consortium governance underwent major restructuring: Agip KCO was replaced by North Caspian Operating Company (NCOC) under consortium-wide arrangements; Kazakhstan government increased its stake (via KMG) through renegotiation; ConocoPhillips exited in 2012. The project is industry-wide referenced as a canonical case of cumulative technical novelty and multi-party governance failure in oil-and-gas megaprojects (Merrow).

## Risk classes that materialised

- **Cumulative technical novelty** — the combination of sour service, shallow ice-covered waters, H₂S reinjection, and specialised metallurgy at scale interacted in ways no single previous development had faced.
- **Optimism bias in capital cost** — cost grew ~5×.
- **Multi-party consortium governance fragmentation** — committee-based decision-making with rotating operator produced accountability diffusion and slow response to emerging issues.
- **Coalition fragmentation under distress** — ConocoPhillips exit and Kazakhstan stake increase reflect consortium instability.
- **Host-government posture shift** — PSA interpretation, tax, local-content, and environmental expectations shifted during distress.
- **Operational reliability failure after delivery** — sulphur-induced cracking post-first-oil reflects metallurgy and sour-service design inadequacy in the as-built system.
- **Supplier capacity constraint** — specialised sour-service equipment procurement created supply-chain bottlenecks.
- **Political time-horizon mismatch** — host-government political environment shifted materially over the extended horizon.
