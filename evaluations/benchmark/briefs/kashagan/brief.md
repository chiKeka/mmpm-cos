# Kashagan — Decision Brief: 2001 Phase 1 Final Investment Decision

## Context at decision moment

The Kashagan field is a supergiant hydrocarbon accumulation in the shallow waters of the northern Caspian Sea, discovered in 2000 by the Offshore Kazakhstan International Operating Company (OKIOC) consortium. Following the discovery, the consortium has developed a Phase 1 development concept for the first-phase production of the field. The consortium composition at this stage includes Eni/Agip (as operator through Agip KCO), ExxonMobil, Shell, Total, ConocoPhillips, Inpex, and BP/Statoil (subsequently partially restructured). Host government relations are managed under the Production Sharing Agreement (PSA) framework with the Republic of Kazakhstan. The development environment combines several challenging features: ultra-sour crude with high hydrogen sulphide (H₂S) content; very shallow waters with extensive winter ice cover; extreme seasonal temperature variation; environmental sensitivity of the northern Caspian ecosystem; and novel integrated offshore-to-onshore processing requirements to manage the H₂S stream. Phase 1 capital cost estimate at FID is approximately $10 billion. First-oil target is 2005.

## The decision

It is 2001. The consortium partners and the Kazakhstan government must decide whether to approve the Phase 1 Final Investment Decision (FID) at the current scope and cost estimate, modify the scope or phasing (for example, defer aspects of integrated processing or revise facilities), or defer FID pending further engineering and cost de-risking. Approval triggers procurement, construction, and offshore installation commitments on an approximately 4-year horizon to first oil.

## What is known

- Field is a supergiant with long-horizon production potential.
- Reservoir conditions include high H₂S content (ultra-sour), high reservoir pressure, and reservoir characteristics that require specialised metallurgy and processing design.
- Northern Caspian is shallow (typical <10 m), with extensive seasonal ice cover that requires ice-resistant offshore facility design.
- Consortium is a large multi-party structure with rotating operator and committee-based decision-making.
- Kazakhstan government is party to the PSA framework and has specific content, procurement, and employment expectations.
- Host-government regulatory environment is relatively early in its oil-and-gas regulatory maturation.
- Phase 1 capital cost estimate: approximately $10 billion.
- First-oil target: 2005.
- No directly comparable development exists combining ultra-sour crude, shallow ice-covered waters, environmental sensitivity, and large-scale H₂S reinjection.
- Integrated offshore production islands connected by sub-sea pipelines to onshore processing are proposed.
- Sulphur-storage and export handling are part of the development scope.
- Agip KCO is nominated as operator.

## What is assumed

- factual_assumption: Phase 1 capital cost estimate of approximately $10bn will hold within standard FID-stage optimism-bias allowances.
- temporal_assumption: First oil can be achieved by 2005 on the planned procurement and construction schedule.
- structural_assumption: Multi-party consortium governance with rotating operator can coordinate engineering, procurement, and construction decisions at the required pace.
- evidence_assumption: Current engineering-stage cost estimates adequately reflect the novel technical challenges of ultra-sour shallow-ice-covered operation.
- behavioural_assumption: Contractor and subcontractor pool has the capability and capacity to deliver specialised metallurgy, sour-service equipment, ice-resistant facilities, and H₂S reinjection on schedule.
- stakeholder_assumption: Host government posture on PSA interpretation, local content, regulatory approvals, and environmental standards will remain stable across the development horizon.
- futures_assumption: Oil price, sulphur market, and project-financing conditions will support the business case.

## What is contested

- Cost realism — among consortium partners there are differing views on cost and schedule achievability.
- Technical novelty cumulatively — whether the combination of sour service, ice-resistance, shallow-water logistics, and H₂S reinjection at this scale is adequately de-risked.
- Integrated-development versus phased/staged approaches.
- Contractor capacity for specialised equipment supply.
- Host-government relationship stability and PSA interpretation longevity.
- Sulphur market and export logistics assumptions.
- Environmental regulation evolution over the development horizon.

## What is unknown

- Behaviour of novel equipment and metallurgy combinations under operating conditions.
- Cost and schedule behaviour of specialised equipment procurement in parallel across multiple tier-1 suppliers.
- Evolution of host-government regulatory, fiscal, and local-content expectations during a long construction horizon.
- Behaviour of multi-party consortium governance under cost or schedule distress.
- Oil-price, sulphur-price, and financing environment across 2001–2015.
- Environmental incident response capability in a sensitive ecosystem.
