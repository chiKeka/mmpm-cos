# Crossrail — Ground Truth

**SEALED — do not open during brief construction or output reading.**

## Outcome summary

On 31 August 2018, Crossrail announced that the December 2018 opening would be delayed, initially referenced to autumn 2019 but subsequently revised multiple times. The central section opened on 24 May 2022 — approximately 3.5 years after the originally declared target. Total cost grew from the £14.8bn envelope toward approximately £19bn at completion. The post-August 2018 disclosure revealed that the on-track status reporting to sponsor boards in mid-2018 had not accurately reflected the integration-testing state. Chairman Sir Terry Morgan resigned in December 2018. Independent reviews (KPMG for TfL, NAO for Parliament) documented assurance failures, programme management weaknesses, and systems-integration underestimation. The eventual operational service is widely regarded as successful; the programme is both a major transport achievement and a textbook late-programme assurance failure.

## Risk classes that materialised

- **Assurance capture** — on-track reporting to sponsor boards did not reflect the integration-testing reality.
- **Tight coupling in late-programme systems integration** — rolling stock, central-section CBTC signalling, surface-section interfaces, station handovers, and operational commissioning were coupled and had no schedule slack.
- **Optimism bias in late-programme scheduling** — confidence in compressed-parallel delivery was not supported by integration-testing evidence.
- **Governance fragmentation in the end-game** — CRL, TfL, DfT, tier-1 contractors, and the systems integrator produced accountability diffusion.
- **Commercial misalignment in tier-1 / systems-integrator / rolling-stock interfaces** — contractual structures did not align incentives during the crucial integration phase.
- **Reporting / assurance independence** — independent assurance channels did not surface the integration-testing reality in time.
- **Political-communication commitment lock-in** — the December 2018 date had been publicly reinforced repeatedly, creating one-way pressure.

## Contemporaneous markers for D2 scoring

Signals identifiable from material available before August 2018:

1. **Schedule compression in final integration phase relative to comparable signalling integrations was internally noted** — visible in programme documentation.
2. **Multiple station handovers had not yet occurred and operational commissioning time was compressed** — visible in programme schedule.
3. **Rolling stock delivery and software issues were documented** — visible in tier-1 and supplier reporting.
4. **CBTC/ETCS handover-zone integration was a known area of technical complexity** — visible in design documentation.
5. **Programme management structure (tier-1 stations + systems integrator + separate rolling-stock supplier) concentrates integration risk** — a structural feature.
6. **Late-programme assurance capture is a well-documented failure mode** in megaproject literature and UK major-programme assurance record.
7. **On-track reporting despite compressed schedule is a reference-class warning signal** — the pattern appears in prior UK rail programmes.
8. **IPA / PRB / sponsor-board review cadence may have been calibrated to earlier programme phases rather than the end-game integration phase**.

A Council output scoring 2 on D2 should identify at least five of the eight.

## Scorer hindsight discipline note

Crossrail mid-2018 is a particularly demanding case for D2 because the on-track reporting was itself a contemporaneous signal. A Council output that treats on-track reporting as self-evidently trustworthy misses the reference-class pattern; one that interrogates the assurance chain and asks for integration-testing evidence is demonstrating the relevant reasoning.
