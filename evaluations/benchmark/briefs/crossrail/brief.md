# Crossrail (Elizabeth Line) — Decision Brief: Mid-2018 final assurance commitment to December 2018 opening

## Context at decision moment

Crossrail is a new east-west railway for London comprising approximately 118 km of line with 41 stations, including a new central tunnel section under central London between Paddington and Whitechapel/Canary Wharf. Sponsors are Transport for London (TfL) and the Department for Transport (DfT). The delivery organisation is Crossrail Limited (CRL), a wholly-owned subsidiary of TfL. The hybrid bill received royal assent in 2008; construction began in 2009. The programme has reported publicly that services through the central section will commence in December 2018 with phased openings of the full route to follow. Rolling stock (Bombardier Aventra Class 345) is being delivered; signalling comprises an interface between TPWS/AWS on surface sections, ETCS on Great Western and Great Eastern routes, and a CBTC system in the central tunnel section. Stations are being handed over from tier-1 construction contractors to CRL for systems integration and operational commissioning. Chief Executive is Andrew Wolstenholme (succeeded by Simon Wright in early 2018). Programme board chair is Sir Terry Morgan. Current cost envelope is approximately £14.8 billion.

## The decision

It is mid-2018. The Crossrail programme board, TfL, and DfT must assess whether to confirm the December 2018 opening of the central section as planned, defer the opening publicly, or defer the decision itself pending further integrated-testing evidence. The decision has implications for operational readiness, rolling-stock delivery acceptance, signalling integration testing, station-handover sequencing, commercial arrangements with operators, and public communications.

## What is known

- Programme has consistently reported publicly that the December 2018 opening is achievable.
- Rolling stock delivery has experienced issues; final units and software configurations remain in progress.
- The central-section CBTC signalling system is in integrated testing with the Aventra stock.
- Several stations have not yet been handed over from construction contractors to CRL for operational commissioning.
- Interface between the central-section CBTC and the Great Western ETCS / Great Eastern signalling systems requires integrated testing across the handover zones.
- The programme management structure uses tier-1 station contractors, a systems-integration contract, and a separate rolling-stock supplier (Bombardier).
- Cost envelope has grown during the programme from earlier estimates.
- Sponsor boards and PRB (Project Representative) have received programme reports through the programme life indicating on-track status.
- Independent assurance (IPA / DfT senior oversight, NAO scheduled review) is in place.
- Schedule compression in the final integration phase relative to comparable signalling integrations elsewhere has been noted internally.

## What is assumed

- factual_assumption: Remaining rolling-stock software and signalling integration can be resolved within the remaining calendar time to December 2018.
- temporal_assumption: Station handovers, systems integration, trial running, trial operations, and driver training can proceed in compressed parallel without material rework.
- structural_assumption: The programme management structure (tier-1 stations, systems integrator, rolling-stock supplier, CRL) can coordinate the end-game phase effectively.
- evidence_assumption: On-track programme reporting to sponsor boards reflects the true integration-testing state.
- behavioural_assumption: Tier-1 and systems contractors will sustain required pace during final integration.
- stakeholder_assumption: TfL and DfT sponsors will receive adequate transparency on issues to act if the timeline becomes implausible.

## What is contested

- Whether the December 2018 opening can be achieved with operational and safety integrity intact.
- Whether on-track reporting to sponsor boards reflects the true state of signalling and rolling-stock integration.
- Whether the compressed parallel final-integration approach embeds rework risk.
- Whether station handover sequencing allows adequate operational commissioning.
- Whether the CBTC/ETCS/surface-signalling handover-zone integration has been adequately de-risked.
- Whether cost envelope is adequate through to revenue operations.

## What is unknown

- True state of end-to-end integrated testing under operational conditions.
- Residual defect density in rolling stock and signalling.
- Behaviour of the integrated system under planned trial-running scenarios not yet executed.
- Sensitivity of December 2018 opening viability to plausible adverse emergence in remaining integration.
- Whether late-programme disclosure of integration issues will occur in time for sponsor-level response.

## The question posed to the three conditions
