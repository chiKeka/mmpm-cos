# Edmonton Valley Line Southeast LRT — Decision Brief: 2013 scope-and-delivery-model commitment

## Context at decision moment

The Valley Line Southeast (VLSE) is a proposed 13-kilometre low-floor urban LRT line running from downtown Edmonton southeast through the Mill Woods neighbourhood, including at-grade running in urban corridors, an elevated section across the North Saskatchewan River valley, and approximately 11 stations. The City of Edmonton is sponsor. The line extends a broader Valley Line vision eventually connecting southeast and west Edmonton. A conventional public-works delivery and a public-private partnership (P3) delivery approach are both under evaluation. A Value for Money (VfM) analysis comparing P3 Design-Build-Finance-Operate-Maintain (DBFOM) against a Public Sector Comparator has been developed. PPP Canada, the federal P3 Crown corporation, is a potential funding partner contingent on P3 delivery structure. Project capital envelope is approximately CAD $1.8 billion. Concession period under the P3 structure would be approximately 30 years. Technology is proposed as low-floor urban LRT, distinct from the existing Edmonton Capital and Metro high-floor LRT lines. Planned opening is December 2020.

## The decision

It is 2013. Edmonton City Council must decide whether to commit to P3 DBFOM delivery for the Valley Line Southeast as recommended, pursue conventional public delivery, or defer the decision pending further analysis. A commitment to P3 triggers procurement through a Request for Qualifications / Request for Proposals process, continued engagement with PPP Canada on funding, and sequenced approvals at Council and provincial levels.

## What is known

- Capital envelope: approximately CAD $1.8 billion.
- Delivery options evaluated: P3 DBFOM vs Public Sector Comparator.
- VfM analysis, based on standard Canadian Treasury Board-style methodology, indicates a positive VfM for the P3 option — driven by risk transfer valuation and claimed operating-phase efficiencies.
- PPP Canada funding contribution is contingent on P3 structure.
- Technology: low-floor urban LRT, operationally separate from Edmonton's existing high-floor LRT network.
- Alignment includes urban at-grade running with adjacent road traffic, an elevated river-valley crossing, and a mix of side-running and median-running configurations.
- Stations: approximately 11, with a mix of street-level and platform configurations.
- Concession period: approximately 30 years under P3 structure.
- Target opening: December 2020.
- Canadian P3 reference class includes Confederation Line (Ottawa), Canada Line (Vancouver), Eglinton Crosstown (Toronto, in preparation), Waterloo ION (in preparation), and earlier Canadian P3 transit experiences.
- Edmonton Transit Service operates the existing LRT network and would operate VLSE (or retain operations depending on DBFOM scope interpretation).

## What is assumed

- factual_assumption: VfM analysis captures the relevant costs and risks comparably between P3 and PSC.
- factual_assumption: Risk-transfer valuation underlying the positive VfM reflects the risks actually transferred in practice to the P3 consortium.
- temporal_assumption: A December 2020 opening is achievable within the P3 delivery horizon.
- structural_assumption: City governance can manage a 30-year DBFOM concession alongside operation of the existing LRT network.
- behavioural_assumption: P3 consortium incentives align with urban-LRT operational quality, winter-climate reliability, and integration with the existing network.
- evidence_assumption: Canadian low-floor urban LRT in winter-climate conditions has sufficient reference-class operational data to support efficiency and reliability assumptions.
- stakeholder_assumption: Affected urban-corridor residents, businesses, and neighbourhood groups will accept at-grade running impacts on the scale planned.
- futures_assumption: Ridership, property value, and urban-development assumptions underpinning the case will be realised.

## What is contested

- Whether VfM risk-transfer valuation realistically reflects return-of-risk scenarios under distress.
- Whether the P3 capital-efficiency assumption is supported by the Canadian P3 reference class.
- Whether the 30-year concession structure produces operational-interface friction with Edmonton Transit Service and the existing high-floor LRT network.
- Whether low-floor urban LRT in at-grade urban-corridor operation is optimal for the alignment's operational context.
- Whether the elevated river-valley crossing design and at-grade urban running produce cumulative novel-integration risk.
- Whether the December 2020 opening target is consistent with comparable P3 urban-LRT schedules.

## What is unknown

- Behaviour of the P3 contract structure under late-programme integration distress.
- Cost and schedule behaviour of the cumulative technical and urban-integration challenges.
- Evolution of stakeholder posture across a 30-year concession horizon.
- Climate and operational reliability of low-floor urban LRT in Edmonton winter conditions.
- Political continuity across successive City Council terms.
- Interaction between the VLSE concession and future expansion (Valley Line West and beyond).
- Consortium financial stability across a 30-year concession given private-sector counterparty evolution.
