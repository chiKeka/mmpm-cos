# Edmonton Valley Line SE — Ground Truth

**SEALED — do not open during brief construction or output reading.**

## Outcome summary

City Council committed to P3 DBFOM delivery in 2013. TransEd Partners (consortium including Bechtel, EllisDon, Bombardier, Fengate) was selected in 2016 as preferred proponent and achieved Financial Close. Construction proceeded through the late 2010s. Originally scheduled to open in December 2020, the Valley Line Southeast opened on 4 November 2022 — approximately 23 months late. The delay was driven principally by signalling system integration issues and defects requiring rectification. Post-opening operational issues have included intermittent signalling failures and stop-spacing performance questions. Capital cost remained broadly within envelope on reported figures though penalty mechanisms and contractual disputes affected the commercial position. The line is operationally functional. The project is a mixed-outcome case, materially under-performing on schedule and early operational reliability while ultimately delivering the scoped infrastructure.

## Risk classes that materialised

- **Late-programme systems integration and tight coupling** — signalling, vehicle, civil, and operational commissioning were coupled with inadequate late-programme slack.
- **Assurance capture / commercial incentive interaction** — the independent certifier and contract assurance chain did not surface integration issues in time for schedule preservation.
- **P3 risk-transfer realism under distress** — risks formally transferred under the concession structure nonetheless produced delivery distress that the sponsor (City) had to manage operationally and reputationally.
- **Technology integration risk** — low-floor urban LRT operating at-grade alongside road traffic, combined with novel signalling and control systems, produced integration complexity.
- **Schedule optimism bias** — the December 2020 target was optimistic relative to comparable Canadian P3 transit schedules.
- **Stakeholder fragmentation** — neighbourhoods, business associations, and different City departments introduced coordination friction.
- **Reputational / legitimacy exposure to the City** — delivery performance affected Edmonton's transit reputation and public trust, notwithstanding contractual risk allocation.
- **Governance under commercial distress** — the City's latitude to intervene during the delay phase was constrained by the DBFOM concession structure.

Signals identifiable from material available in 2013:

1. **Canadian P3 transit reference class was available** — Canada Line (Vancouver), Confederation Line (Ottawa, in preparation), Eglinton Crosstown — offering base-rate data on schedule performance.
2. **P3 risk-transfer literature critiquing return-of-risk under distress was publishable and known** (Siemiatycki, Vining & Boardman).
3. **Low-floor urban LRT at-grade in cold climate had limited Canadian reference class** — a novelty marker.
4. **30-year concession horizon exceeds typical political-continuity and counterparty-stability horizons** — structural marker.
5. **VfM analysis treating risk transfer as point-estimate without distress modelling** is a known structural pattern in Canadian Treasury Board VfM — identifiable in the analysis documentation.
6. **Cumulative integration complexity (at-grade urban + elevated river valley + novel signalling + operational interface with existing network)** was a structural feature visible in the scope.
7. **Target December 2020 opening is compressed relative to comparable Canadian P3 transit programmes** — reference-class schedule marker.
8. **Operational interface between VLSE and ETS / existing network creates split-accountability risk** — a structural feature.
9. **Canadian P3 transit programmes have documented late-programme assurance gaps** in prior instances — reference-class pattern.
