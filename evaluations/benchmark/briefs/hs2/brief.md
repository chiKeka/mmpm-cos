# HS2 Phase 1 — Decision Brief: January 2012 route confirmation

## Context at decision moment

High Speed 2 (HS2) is a proposed new high-speed rail network for Great Britain. HS2 Ltd, a company limited by guarantee, has been established as the programme sponsor, reporting to the Department for Transport (DfT). The route concept comprises a Y-network: Phase 1 from London Euston to Birmingham with a spur to HS1 at St Pancras; Phase 2 in two legs from Birmingham to Manchester (western) and to Leeds (eastern). A public consultation on Phase 1 closed in July 2011. The Secretary of State for Transport, Justine Greening, is expected to announce the government's decision on Phase 1 route confirmation in January 2012. An environmental statement and formal hybrid-bill process will follow route confirmation, with a target royal assent in 2016–17 and construction start in 2017.

## The decision

It is January 2012. The Secretary of State must decide whether to confirm the Phase 1 route (London–Birmingham) as consulted upon, modify it in response to consultation responses, pursue an alternative (for example, classic network upgrade), or defer the decision for further work. Confirmation triggers the hybrid-bill preparatory phase, further environmental and engineering work, and a commitment of departmental and HS2 Ltd resources on the Phase 1 schedule. The decision also signals intent with respect to Phase 2.

## What is known

- HS2 Ltd business case estimates total Y-network capital cost at approximately £32.7bn (2011 prices), of which Phase 1 is approximately £17bn.
- Stated benefit–cost ratio (BCR) for the Y-network is approximately 2.0 under standard assumptions, rising with wider economic benefits (WEBs) assumptions.
- Projected passenger demand growth underpins the business case; the DfT's demand model produces forecasts higher than critics' alternative models.
- Travel-time savings form a principal component of benefits; the assumption that in-train business time has zero productive value is embedded in the standard WebTAG methodology.
- Environmental designations along the proposed Phase 1 alignment include the Chilterns Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty (AONB).
- Organised opposition is established, including HS2 Action Alliance, local authorities along the route, and affected residents.
- Parliamentary opposition includes Conservative MPs in affected constituencies.
- International comparators (France TGV, Japan Shinkansen, Spain AVE, Germany ICE) provide reference-class data on high-speed rail programmes.
- A Major Project Review Group (MPRG) process within government applies to programmes of this scale.

## What is assumed

- factual_assumption: Capital cost estimate of £17bn (Phase 1) will hold within standard optimism-bias uplift allowances.
- futures_assumption: Rail demand will continue growing at rates consistent with HS2 Ltd's demand model.
- structural_assumption: Phase 1 can be delivered under the proposed governance structure (HS2 Ltd as delivery company, DfT as sponsor, hybrid-bill parliamentary process).
- temporal_assumption: The 2017–2026 construction schedule is achievable.
- behavioral_assumption: Stakeholder opposition will be containable through consultation, design refinement, and mitigation.
- evidence_assumption: The value of business travel time savings and the zero-productivity-on-train assumption reflect reasonable methodology.
- stakeholder_assumption: Local opposition will not materially delay or reshape the parliamentary process.

## What is contested

- Demand model reliability — alternative forecasts suggest substantially lower ridership.
- Business travel time valuation — critics argue in-train work (laptops, mobile telephony) makes the zero-value assumption indefensible.
- Cost realism — some commentators cite international cost comparisons and the UK's track record on rail capital projects as reasons to expect substantial escalation.
- Network alternative — critics argue classic network upgrades and capacity enhancements (signalling, rolling stock, selected realignments) could meet most of the capacity objective at lower cost.
- Environmental impact on the Chilterns AONB is contested between tunnel mitigation proposals.
- Benefit distribution — whether Phase 1 primarily benefits London relative to the Midlands and North is contested.

## What is unknown

- Cost and schedule behaviour of the geotechnical and engineering challenges along the full Phase 1 alignment under detailed design.
- Cost and schedule behaviour of Phase 2 (not yet consulted upon in detail).
- Political continuity across the 2015 general election and beyond.
- Evolution of rail demand patterns over the 2017–2040 construction and ramp-up horizon.
- Sensitivity of the BCR to plausible adverse movements in cost, demand, and benefit-valuation assumptions.
