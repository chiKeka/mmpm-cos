# Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER) — Decision Brief: Q1 2012 opening commitment

## Context at decision moment

The new Berlin Brandenburg Airport (Willy Brandt) is a consolidated airport replacing Berlin's Tegel and Schönefeld operations, built adjacent to the existing Schönefeld site. The programme is owned by Flughafen Berlin Brandenburg GmbH (FBB), jointly held by the Federal Republic of Germany, the State of Berlin, and the State of Brandenburg. Construction began in 2006 following extended planning approval proceedings. The initial targeted opening was October 2011. Following delays, the opening was rescheduled to 3 June 2012. FBB's Technical Director Manfred Körtgen was dismissed in 2010; Rainer Schwarz is CEO. Airlines — principally Lufthansa and Air Berlin — have scheduled operations from BER commencing 3 June 2012 and have begun passenger-facing communications and operational preparations accordingly.

## The decision

It is Q1 2012. The FBB Supervisory Board must decide whether to reaffirm the 3 June 2012 opening date, announce a deferral, or defer the decision pending resolution of outstanding commissioning items. A reaffirmation triggers final operational readiness, airline commitments, tenant activation, and regulatory sign-off sequencing. A deferral implies contractual exposure to airlines, tenants, and the Berlin-Brandenburg regional economy, plus reputational exposure for the three public shareholders.

## What is known

- The fire protection and smoke extraction system (Brandschutzanlage) in the main terminal has not yet passed final commissioning tests.
- TÜV, the independent technical certifier, has raised preliminary issues regarding the integration of the smoke extraction system with the building's control architecture.
- The fire protection contractor scope spans multiple subcontractors with complex interface responsibility.
- The programme has absorbed scope expansion during construction including terminal capacity upgrades and retail expansion.
- Passenger volume forecasts for BER assume consolidation of Tegel and Schönefeld traffic plus growth.
- Multiple preceding opening-date commitments have been made and then revised.
- The FBB organisational structure has seen turnover in technical leadership during construction.
- Tegel's operating licence is structured in relation to BER's opening date.

## What is assumed

- factual_assumption: Outstanding fire protection items can be cleared to TÜV's satisfaction within weeks.
- structural_assumption: The FBB governance structure (three public shareholders, tripartite board) can converge on a single opening-date position.
- temporal_assumption: Fire system commissioning and airline operational readiness can proceed on parallel tracks without emerging interdependencies.
- behavioral_assumption: Airlines will absorb residual risk of late operational changes at stated commitments.
- evidence_assumption: Current contractor and subcontractor representations about completion timelines are reliable.

## What is contested

- Whether the fire protection system can be certified in the stated timeframe — internal engineering views and TÜV preliminary findings differ in emphasis.
- Whether the interface between the smoke extraction control system and the terminal's broader building management system is resolvable without substantive rework.
- Whether subcontractor reporting accurately reflects on-site condition.
- Whether the current commissioning test regime is appropriately designed for a terminal of this scale and complexity.

## What is unknown

- The full extent of rework required if the current commissioning approach does not achieve certification.
- The financial, contractual, and political consequences of a further deferral at this stage.
- The stability of airline operational commitments under alternative opening-date scenarios.

## The question posed to the three conditions

> You are an advisor to the programme board. The board is meeting in Q1 2012 to decide whether to reaffirm the 3 June 2012 opening date for Berlin Brandenburg Airport, defer the opening to a later date, or defer the decision itself pending further technical review. What is your analysis? What would you recommend, with what confidence, and why?
