# BER — Ground Truth

**SEALED — do not open during brief construction or during Stage 1–2 output reading. Open only at scoring audit.**

## Outcome summary

The 3 June 2012 opening was cancelled on 8 May 2012 — weeks before the scheduled opening — following continued failure of the fire protection and smoke extraction system to achieve certification. Subsequent opening-date commitments were made and then revised through 2013–2019. The airport eventually opened on 31 October 2020, approximately nine years after the original October 2011 target. Total construction cost rose from early-programme estimates of approximately €2bn to around €6–7bn depending on scope of inclusion. FBB leadership turned over multiple times post-2012 (Schwarz dismissed 2013; Mehdorn 2013–2015; Mühlenfeld 2015–2017; Lütke Daldrup 2017–2021). The Bundesrechnungshof and Berliner Rechnungshof published multiple reports critical of governance, shareholder oversight, and procurement.

## Risk classes that materialised (against methodology.md §4 D2 taxonomy)

- **Escalation of commitment** — multiple opening-date announcements followed by reaffirmation despite accumulating technical concerns.
- **Tight coupling** — fire protection / smoke extraction / building management system / airline readiness / tenant activation were interdependent with inadequate interface ownership.
- **Governance fragmentation** — tripartite public shareholder structure (Bund, Berlin, Brandenburg) with diffused accountability and slow crisis response.
- **Novel-technology / complex-integration risk** — smoke extraction system design was novel in scale and integration complexity; contractor capability was overestimated.
- **Commercial misalignment** — multiple subcontractors with unclear interface responsibility; fire protection contractor (Imtech Deutschland) subsequently entered insolvency.
- **Assurance capture / assurance weakness** — optimistic commissioning reporting was taken at face value by governance bodies.
- **Political time-horizon mismatch** — opening-date commitments aligned with political cycles and airline schedules, not technical completion confidence.

## Contemporaneous markers for D2 scoring

The following signals were identifiable from material available before the Q1 2012 decision:

1. **Multiple prior opening-date commitments had already been revised** — the track record of schedule reliability on this programme was poor at the decision moment.
2. **Technical leadership turnover during construction** — Körtgen's 2010 dismissal was public; organisational memory of technical issues was disrupted.
3. **Scope expansion during construction was documented** — increases in terminal capacity and retail area were reported in German press and FBB reporting.
4. **TÜV preliminary concerns about smoke extraction system integration were documented in late 2011 / early 2012** — the certifier's position was not one of confident imminent certification.
5. **Subcontractor interface fragmentation in fire protection scope** — visible in contract documentation and contractor reporting.
6. **Tripartite shareholder governance structure with documented prior examples of slow convergence on decisions** — the governance fragility was a structural feature, not news.
7. **Airline readiness commitments and Tegel licensing structure created one-way pressure toward announcing an opening** — the incentive asymmetry was visible.
8. **Cost trajectory from initial estimates to 2012** already exhibited the classic infrastructure cost-growth pattern Flyvbjerg's reference class forecasting identifies.

A Council output scoring 2 on D2 should identify at least five of the above eight. A vanilla output scoring 2 should also — the markers are contemporaneously visible and identifiable by a careful reader of the brief, not requiring outcome knowledge.
