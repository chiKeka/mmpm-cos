# Sydney Opera House — Ground Truth

**SEALED — do not open during brief construction or output reading. Open only at scoring audit.**

## Outcome summary

Stage 1 (podium) construction commenced in March 1959 before the shell geometry was resolved. The shell geometry was eventually rationalised as spherical segments in 1961, requiring substantial rework of the podium and design reinterpretation. Stage 2 (shells) construction extended through the 1960s. Utzon resigned in February 1966 after political and management conflict with the incoming Askin government. Stage 3 (interiors, stage machinery, finishes) was completed by a domestic team without Utzon's involvement; the interior functional design departed substantially from Utzon's intentions. The Opera House opened in October 1973 — approximately 10 years later than the original target. Final construction cost was approximately A$102 million against the original £3.5M / ~A$7M estimate — a cost growth of ~1,400%. The building is regarded as an architectural success and UNESCO World Heritage site.

## Risk classes that materialised

- **Scope and design creep** — the external form was fixed before the interior functional design was resolved, forcing scope evolution.
- **Novel-technology / engineering integration** — shell geometry was unprecedented; engineering resolution required multi-year iteration.
- **Governance–political coupling** — Cahill's original patronage, successor governments' differing postures, and ultimately the Askin government's conflict with Utzon illustrate patron-dependence.
- **Sequencing risk** — Stage 1 construction before Stage 2 design caused rework.
- **Commercial misalignment** — procurement and contract structures were not adequate for a programme of this novelty.
- **Optimism bias in initial cost and schedule** — the estimate preceded engineering resolution.
- **Legitimacy / reputation tension** — cost-growth controversy coexisted with architectural acclaim.

## Contemporaneous markers for D2 scoring

Signals identifiable from material available in mid-1957:

1. **Shell geometry was explicitly unresolved in the competition entry** — not an emergent discovery.
2. **Ove Arup's engineering team had raised questions about shell constructibility** — documented in correspondence.
3. **Cost estimate was a scheme-stage estimate before engineering resolution** — a standard warning signal.
4. **Stage 1 construction preceding Stage 2 design would imply rework risk** if Stage 2 design departed from competition-scheme assumptions.
5. **Political timing to precede the March 1959 election** was visible pressure on schedule.
6. **International reference class for novel-form public buildings of this scale** was limited — the novelty itself was a marker.
7. **Interior functional requirements (stage machinery, acoustics, back-of-house) were unresolved** at scheme stage.
8. **Financing via the Opera House Lottery rather than general taxation** was a structural innovation with unknown behaviour across changing political patronage.

A Council output scoring 2 on D2 should identify at least five of the eight.

## Scorer hindsight discipline note

The Sydney Opera House case is complicated by its eventual cultural success. D2 and the rubric measure whether the output identifies the decision-moment risks and reasons under uncertainty — not whether it retrospectively endorses what the programme became. "Proceed but expect cost overruns and a 10-year delay" would score differently from "proceed with explicit acknowledgement that the scheme-to-construction path has unresolved risks warranting formal escalation criteria."
