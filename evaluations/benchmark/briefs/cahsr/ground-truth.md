# California HSR — Ground Truth

**SEALED — do not open during brief construction or output reading. Open only at scoring audit.**

## Outcome summary

Proposition 1A passed on 4 November 2008 with approximately 52.6% support. CHSRA subsequently revised the Business Plan multiple times. Capital cost estimates escalated: from the 2008 ~$33.6bn figure to ~$68bn (2012), ~$77bn (2018), ~$80–100bn+ (post-2020 estimates, depending on scope and whether full Phase 1 to Anaheim is included). Federal HSR funding arrived at a smaller scale than implied. Private investment did not materialise on the scale assumed. Phase 1 scope was effectively restructured; a truncated Initial Operating Section Central Valley alignment (Merced–Bakersfield) became the operational focus. Full Phase 1 opening timelines were pushed beyond 2030 with full San Francisco–Anaheim service not expected within the original horizon. The no-operating-subsidy statutory requirement became the subject of ongoing legal and financial analysis. Multiple CEOs and board changes occurred.

## Risk classes that materialised

- **Optimism bias** — capital cost grew by multiples; ridership forecasts were not delivered within the original horizon.
- **Strategic misrepresentation (as Flyvbjerg defines it)** — cost and ridership figures presented to voters have been credibly argued to reflect sponsor-authoring incentives rather than analytical neutrality.
- **Political time-horizon mismatch** — Phase 1 horizon extended beyond multiple gubernatorial, legislative, and federal administrations.
- **Benefit-case fragility** — ridership, operating-surplus, and federal-funding assumptions all deteriorated.
- **Legitimacy erosion** — Central Valley communities, environmental groups, and fiscal critics shifted from initial support to substantial opposition over time.
- **Stakeholder fragmentation** — inter-jurisdictional alignment decisions (Pacheco, Central Valley stations, Bay Area approaches) produced enduring friction.
- **Governance fragmentation** — CHSRA relationship to state legislature, federal programme, and local jurisdictions was structurally fragmented.

## Contemporaneous markers for D2 scoring

Signals identifiable from material available before November 2008:

1. **International HSR reference class had documented cost escalation patterns** — Flyvbjerg's reference-class work was published and accessible.
2. **Published ridership critiques existed at the time** — Reason Foundation and academic critics documented alternative forecasts.
3. **No completed HSR system existed in the US** — domestic delivery experience reference class was empty.
4. **220 mph specification exceeded international service norms** — a readily observable reference-class departure.
5. **Statutory no-operating-subsidy requirement coupled to contested revenue forecasts** — a structural fragility visible in the measure text.
6. **Federal HSR programme was novel and pre-ARRA** — reliance on a funding stream that did not yet exist at scale.
7. **CHSRA delivery-organisation scale was small relative to programme scale** — visible in CHSRA staffing and budget.
8. **Inter-jurisdictional alignment decisions were known to be unresolved** — Pacheco vs Altamont and Central Valley stations were public disputes.
9. **2008 Business Plan contingency levels were below typical megaproject best-practice** — visible in the plan.

A Council output scoring 2 on D2 should identify at least five of the nine.
