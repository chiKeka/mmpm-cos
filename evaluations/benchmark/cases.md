# Benchmark Case List

**Status**: DRAFT v0 — proposed list awaiting user verification.

## Selection criteria

1. **Public documentation** — sufficient contemporaneous and retrospective material to construct a hindsight-stripped decision brief.
2. **Consequential decision moment** — a clear go / no-go or scope-commitment event, not a slow drift.
3. **Coverage of MMPM risk classes** — collectively the cases should surface optimism bias, tight coupling, stakeholder fragmentation, political time-horizon mismatch, escalation of commitment, novel-technology risk, legitimacy erosion, and commercial misalignment.
4. **Outcome variance** — not only spectacular failures. Includes two relative successes to test whether Council produces appropriately tempered analysis, not just well-calibrated alarm.
5. **Sector spread** — aviation, rail, road, events, oil & gas, cultural infrastructure, so findings don't reduce to "rail is hard."
6. **Geographic spread** — avoid Anglosphere-only sample.

## Proposed cases (8)

| # | Case | Decision moment | Primary risk class tested | Outcome direction |
|---|------|-----------------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER)** | 2006 commitment to 2011 opening after multiple delays; commitment to proceed to opening in 2012 despite known defect list | Escalation of commitment, tight coupling, governance fragmentation | Major failure (9 years late, ~3× cost) |
| 2 | **California High-Speed Rail (Phase 1)** | 2008 Proposition 1A bond authorization and early 2010s route-commitment decisions | Optimism bias, political time-horizon mismatch, legitimacy erosion | Ongoing failure mode |
| 3 | **HS2 (UK)** | 2012 Phase 1 route confirmation; 2020 Oakervee review commit-or-cancel | Optimism bias, stakeholder fragmentation, cost-estimate discipline | Major failure (Phase 2 cancelled, Phase 1 cost tripled) |
| 4 | **Sydney Opera House** | 1957 scheme commitment before design resolution; 1962 shell-design commitment | Scope creep, novel-technology integration, governance-political coupling | Cost/schedule failure, cultural success |
| 5 | **Big Dig (Boston Central Artery/Tunnel)** | 1985 federal approval and scope-commitment decisions on immersed-tube tunnel design | Tight coupling, commercial misalignment, governance fragmentation | Major failure on cost/schedule, partial delivery success |
| 6 | **Crossrail (Elizabeth Line)** | 2007 hybrid-bill commitment; 2015–2018 "on track" assurance decisions before opening-slip disclosure | Optimism bias late in programme, assurance capture, tight coupling of systems integration | Relative success eventually, but late-programme failure mode |
| 7 | **Kashagan oil field development** | 2001 phase-1 Final Investment Decision by consortium | Novel-technology integration risk, commercial misalignment, coalition fragmentation | Major failure on cost/schedule |
| 8 | **Edmonton Valley Line (Southeast) LRT** | 2013 scope-and-delivery-model commitment (P3 DBFOM) | P3 risk allocation, urban-transit political economy, local relevance | Relative underperformance (~2 years late, quality issues at opening) |

## Alternates considered and not included

- **Channel Tunnel** — rich, but main decision moment (1986 treaty) is long-horizon; tighter decision-moment framing is difficult.
- **Montreal Olympics (1976)** — excellent optimism bias case but events infrastructure is a different class from sustained-operations megaprojects; may dilute the risk-class taxonomy.
- **Boston Big Dig tunnel-collapse decision (2006 post-failure)** — tempting for accountability analysis, but sits post-opening and doesn't fit the "decision under contemporaneous uncertainty" frame.

If a proposed case is unavailable for source reasons, substitute from alternates in order: Channel Tunnel (1986), Montreal Olympics (1970 IOC bid commitment), Denver International Airport (baggage-system 1992 commitment).

## Source strategy

For each case:
1. Pre-decision sources — news and official documents dated before the decision moment.
2. Inquiry / audit reports — NAO, state auditors, parliamentary inquiries, royal commissions, academic case studies.
3. Flyvbjerg case library where applicable.
4. At least one non-Anglophone source for non-UK/US cases where possible.

Sources will be logged per case in `benchmark/briefs/{case}.sources.md`.
