# Advice to the Programme Board — Crossrail Central Section, December 2018 Opening

## My headline view

Do not confirm the December 2018 opening at this meeting. Defer the public opening date now, on your own terms, and commission an independent integrated-testing evidence review before setting a revised date. My confidence in this recommendation is high. My confidence that a December 2018 opening is actually achievable with operational and safety integrity intact is low.

I recognise this is an uncomfortable recommendation. The programme has reported on-track publicly and to sponsors for an extended period, contracts, commercial arrangements with operators, and public expectations are all anchored on December 2018, and the reputational cost of moving the date is real and immediate. I have weighed that. The cost of holding the date and failing against it in the autumn — or worse, opening and then retreating — is materially greater, and the evidence you have in front of you does not support the continuing public commitment.

## How I read the situation

The pattern in what you have described is, in my experience, a recognisable late-stage major-programme pattern, and it is the pattern that most often precedes a disorderly schedule failure rather than a successful landing.

The specific signals that concern me, taken together rather than individually:

1. **The work left is integration work, not construction work.** Rolling-stock software not finalised, CBTC still in integrated testing with the stock, handover-zone signalling integration across three signalling regimes still to prove out, and several stations not yet even handed over to CRL for operational commissioning. Integration work of this kind characteristically uncovers issues that cannot be foreseen from component testing. The calendar between mid-2018 and a December revenue-service opening is not long enough to absorb a normal emergence of integration defects, let alone an adverse one — and you have no basis yet to assume emergence will be normal rather than adverse.

2. **Trial running and trial operations have not yet been executed.** These are the phases that generate the evidence you would need to confirm a December opening with confidence. You cannot confirm the date on the basis of plans to generate that evidence; you can only confirm it on the basis of the evidence itself. Confirming now is confirming on assumption.

3. **Parallelism is being used to recover time.** Compressed parallel execution of station handovers, systems integration, trial running, trial operations, and driver training is a recovery tactic, not a plan. It works only if nothing material goes wrong. It embeds rework risk, because issues found in a later parallel stream often invalidate work already done in an earlier one. The internal note that schedule compression is outside the range of comparable signalling integrations should be read as a warning, not a data point.

4. **There is a credible gap between reported status and actual status.** You have listed this explicitly as contested and unknown. That is itself the finding. When a sponsor board cannot tell whether its reporting reflects reality, the sponsor board has lost the visibility it needs to govern. The appropriate response is not to act on the reporting; it is to restore the visibility.

5. **The delivery structure is fragmented at exactly the point where it needs to be tightest.** Tier-1 station contractors, a separate systems integrator, a separate rolling-stock supplier, and CRL in the middle is a workable structure during construction. In the final integration end-game it is a coordination load that amplifies every small slip. This is the phase in which that structure is weakest.

6. **No meaningful float is visible.** I cannot see, from what is described, any credible schedule contingency between now and December. A programme of this scale and integration complexity entering its final five to six months with no visible float is not a programme on track; it is a programme that has already consumed its contingency and is relying on everything going right from here.

The question you are being asked to answer — "can December 2018 be achieved?" — is the wrong frame. The right frame is: "what evidence would we need to see, and by when, to confirm December 2018 with integrity, and do we have a realistic path to generating that evidence in time?" On the information before you, the honest answer to the second half of that question is no, or at best not yet demonstrated.

## The three options, assessed

**Option A — Confirm December 2018.** I would not support this. You do not have the integrated-testing evidence to stand behind the commitment. The downside is not a schedule slip; it is either a safety-integrity compromise if the date is forced, or a late, disorderly, publicly visible collapse of the date in the autumn when integration evidence forces the issue anyway. The asymmetry is severe. There is little upside to confirming now that you could not capture by confirming later, once evidence supports it.

**Option B — Defer the decision pending further integrated-testing evidence.** Superficially attractive, because it avoids a public date change today. In practice this is the weakest option. It keeps the public December date live while internally acknowledging you do not believe it. That gap — between public commitment and internal assessment — is precisely the governance condition that produces the worst outcomes, because it disincentivises honest escalation from contractors and programme management, and it exposes the sponsors personally if the gap is later disclosed. Do not sit in this position.

**Option C — Defer the opening publicly now, on your terms, and commission an independent integrated-testing evidence review to inform a revised date.** This is my recommendation. It is painful in the short term and correct in the medium term. It re-establishes sponsor control of the narrative, restores honest escalation inside the programme (because there is no longer a date to protect), and puts you in a position to set a revised date from evidence rather than from hope. Announce a deferral without yet committing to a new date; commit to a new date only when integrated testing and trial running support it.

## What I would do alongside the deferral

- Commission an independent technical review of the integrated-testing state — CBTC, ETCS handover zones, rolling-stock software, station operational readiness — reporting to sponsors, not to CRL, within four to six weeks. You need a view of reality that does not pass through the reporting line that has been telling you on-track.
- Ask for a revised, evidence-based schedule that identifies explicit go/no-go gates tied to trial running and trial operations milestones, with visible float.
- Review the cost envelope honestly against the revised schedule. A deferral of this magnitude will consume further cost; better to surface it now in one movement than in successive tranches.
- Review the end-game governance and coordination arrangement. Whatever structure has worked to this point, the integration end-game may need a tighter, more integrated command arrangement across CRL, the systems integrator, and the operator.
- Align communications with TfL, DfT, the Mayor's office, operators, and the supply chain before the announcement, not after. The manner of the deferral matters almost as much as the deferral itself.

## What would change my view

I would revise toward confirming the December date only on the basis of: substantially completed station handovers with operational commissioning underway, integrated CBTC/rolling-stock testing demonstrating stable behaviour over a meaningful period, completed handover-zone signalling integration testing, a credible trial-running programme already in execution with visible float remaining, and an independent view that reporting reflects reality. I do not believe that evidence can realistically be assembled in the time available, but if it is presented, the recommendation changes.

## Confidence and caveats

High confidence in the recommendation to defer publicly now. Low confidence that December 2018 is achievable with integrity. I hold these views on the basis of the pattern described in the brief; I do not have direct sight of the integrated-testing data, and my recommendation is itself partly a response to the fact that the sponsors apparently do not have reliable sight of it either. That is the core governance problem in front of you, and it is the problem the deferral buys you the time and the honesty to fix.
