# Stage 1 — Practitioner VfM Write-up (Baseline)

*Representative competent VfM summary as would be presented to an Alberta Treasury Board review. Drawn from `model/P3_VfM_Model_v2.0.xlsx` and Assumption_Log v2.0. This is the baseline that Council is compared against in Stage 3.*

---

## Executive summary

This Value for Money (VfM) analysis compares delivering a new $220M capital hospital via conventional Design-Build (DB) versus a 33-year P3 Design-Build-Finance-Maintain (DBFM) concession, under the Alberta Treasury Board framework. Based on present-value analysis of capital, operating, lifecycle, and risk-adjusted costs over the concession horizon — and applying the Treasury Board's standard 6% real discount rate — the P3 DBFM structure delivers a positive VfM, driven primarily by a **Risk Transfer Value of $37.2M** and secondary efficiencies in capital cost (–7%) and operating costs (–15%) relative to the PSC comparator.

**Recommendation**: Proceed with P3 DBFM procurement. Undertake market engagement in Q2 with target Financial Close in Q4.

## Approach

The analysis compares total present-value cost to the Government of Alberta (GOA) under two delivery structures over a common 33-year concession period. The Public Sector Comparator (PSC) reflects conventional DB delivery: GOA designs and builds through a single integrated contractor, finances on-balance-sheet, and retains lifecycle and operating risk for the asset's full useful life. The P3 comparator is a DBFM concession under which the Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) designs, builds, finances, and maintains the asset for the 33-year term in exchange for an availability-based service payment.

Cost components compared include base capital cost, contingency, professional fees, operating and lifecycle costs, competitive neutrality adjustments, residual value credits, and the expected value of quantified risks. Financial parameters follow Treasury Board Directive 2019-04 (6.0% real discount rate, 2.0% CPI) and current market benchmarks for P3 financing (85/15 debt-to-equity, 5.5% debt rate, 14% equity target return, 25-year debt term with refinancing in Year 3).

## Cost components

### Capital

Base construction cost of $220M ($180M building, $25M equipment, $15M design) applies in the PSC case with a 10% contingency reflecting Alberta Infrastructure's DB project history, 6% professional fees (integrated design-build), and a 0.95 capital cost factor vs the DBB baseline. In the P3 case, the –7% capital cost efficiency assumption (sourced to the P3 Canada benchmarking study 2022) reflects integrated design-delivery optimisation, whole-life thinking in design choices, and the commercial discipline imposed by external financing. The draw schedule (20%/55%/25% over three years) applies in both cases.

### Operating and lifecycle

PSC operating cost baseline of approximately $8.6M/yr (facilities management $3.5M, utilities $2.0M, lifecycle reserve $1.8M, property management $0.8M, insurance $0.5M) inflates at 2.5% annually. P3 costs are modelled through a base availability payment of $32.36M/yr inclusive of debt service, SPV operating costs, insurance, SPV management fee, and margin; the P3's underlying O&M cost base is modelled at 15% below the PSC equivalent, reflecting private-sector operating efficiency and whole-of-life maintenance discipline.

### Risk

Ten quantified risks with PA clause mappings are modelled at probability × PERT(P10, P50, P90). Aggregate expected value: $45.7M. Under the PSC DB comparator, GOA retains $39.3M; under the P3 DBFM, GOA retains $2.1M — yielding the $37.2M Risk Transfer Value referenced in the executive summary. Risk allocation follows the standard Alberta P3 schedule: construction cost overrun, schedule delay, O&M variance, and lifecycle underestimation transfer fully to the SPV; demand, regulatory, and force majeure risks are shared 50/50; design deficiency, commissioning, and technology obsolescence risks are retained by the P3 SPV under the DBFM structure.

### Residual value

A 40-year asset useful life and $25M appraised land value support a residual value credit applied at handback, net of a 5% handback reserve and 15% wear factor.

## Bankability and sensitivity

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) and equity IRR are checked under the financial structure. Standard lender expectations of minimum DSCR ≥ 1.2× and equity IRR ≥ 12% are evaluated; the scenario supports bankability under current market conditions.

One-way sensitivity on the discount rate (4%–10%) and capital cost variance (–20% to +30%) confirms a positive VfM across all tested ranges. Break-even analysis indicates the discount rate would need to rise to approximately 12% before VfM turns negative — roughly 6 percentage points of headroom. A tornado diagram ranks discount rate, PSC capital cost, operating efficiency, risk allocation, and service payment level as the most sensitive VfM drivers in that order.

## Assumption robustness

Principal assumptions are documented with sources in the Assumption Log:

- Discount rate 6.0% — Alberta TB Directive 2019-04.
- Cost overrun probability 70% — Flyvbjerg reference class forecasting.
- Cost overrun P50 $25M — Alberta historical 5-year average.
- P3 capital efficiency –7% — P3 Canada benchmarking study 2022.
- Equity target return 14% — market comparables (Plenary, PCL, Graham).
- Debt interest rate 5.5% — market survey plus 50 bps construction premium.

## Recommendation

The VfM analysis supports proceeding with a P3 DBFM procurement. The positive VfM is robust across the tested sensitivity ranges, with risk transfer and operating efficiency as the primary drivers. The model's break-even analysis confirms that even without the risk transfer component, P3 capital and operating efficiency gains alone would deliver a positive VfM.

Next steps: issue Request for Qualifications in the current quarter, shortlist in Q3, RFP in Q4, preferred proponent selection in Q2 of the following year, target Financial Close by Q4 Year +1.
