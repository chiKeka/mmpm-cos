# PROGRAMME GOVERNANCE COUNCIL BRIEF

## 1. PROBLEM FRAMING

A transport agency board is being briefed on a $2.4B light rail extension with cost stated at ±5%, schedule at 48 months from CPM, ridership of 80,000 daily within 18 months ramping to 120,000 by year 5, operating cost 15% below network average, BCR 1.8. The board is being asked to assess these inputs. The Chair classifies as CROSS_DOMAIN with implicit GOVERNANCE_DECISION (board ratification of the case is the next step). The brief presents itself with high stated confidence; the analytical question is whether that confidence is supported.

## 4. KEY FINDINGS BY DOMAIN

### 4.1 Risk Agent

The ±5% confidence interval on a $2.4B pre-FC light rail estimate is the headline marker of **optimism bias**. Reference-class data on rail megaprojects (Flyvbjerg et al.) shows mean cost overrun of approximately 40-45% with substantial right-tail dispersion; ±5% implies the estimator believes this project is roughly an order of magnitude more predictable than its reference class. The schedule estimate (48 months via CPM) is similarly point-estimate-shaped; rail-megaproject schedule slippage averages 20%+ on a base-rate basis. The ridership forecast (80,000 in 18 months ramping to 120,000) sits at the optimistic end of the rail-ridership-forecast distribution, which empirically over-predicts demand by 30-50% on average internationally. The fact that the four-step model was produced in-house by the agency on its own extension is a known optimism-bias configuration. The "same methodology used on three prior projects" is reassurance only if those projects landed within ±5% of pre-FC estimates; that track record is not given. Confidence: high that the BCR 1.8 is fragile under joint adverse movement of cost, schedule, and demand.

### 4.2 Commercial Agent

Operating cost 15% below the existing network is the second contestable claim. New alignments, new fleets, and new depot arrangements typically run *above* legacy unit costs in early years, not below. The operating-cost saving requires explicit sourcing.

### 4.3 Strategic Leadership

The director's three-prior-project record is an asset only to the extent the prior projects are reference-class-comparable in scale. A director who delivered three smaller extensions does not necessarily de-bias an estimate for a programme of this scale.

### 4.4 Futures Agent

Demand evolution over the 5-year ramp horizon depends on land-use, fare policy, modal-shift trends, and post-pandemic travel patterns. None of these are pinned in the brief. The 120,000-by-year-5 figure is a planning assumption requiring sensitivity analysis.

## 5. CROSS-DOMAIN INTERACTIONS

### 5.1 Interface Integrity

Cost, schedule, and ridership are presented as independent estimates. They are not — they share a common optimism source (the agency's planning division) and joint adverse movement is the relevant scenario for BCR robustness, not one-way sensitivity.

### 5.2 Emergence & Fragility

BCR 1.8 looks robust under one-way sensitivity. Under joint adverse stress (cost +30%, demand −30%, operating saving collapses), BCR can fall below 1.0. The headline ratio is fragile; this is the central finding.

### 5.3 Constraint & Tradeoff

The brief implicitly treats the case as either-approve-as-presented or reject. The realistic tradeoff is between approval-as-presented and approval-with-conditions (independent peer review, reference-class forecast, downside-scenario BCR).

## 6. ADVERSARIAL REVIEW FINDINGS

### 6.1 Contrarian

Counter-case: the director's track record is genuine evidence; the agency's bottom-up estimating methodology may be more accurate than reference-class averages because it is project-specific; ±5% may reflect mature design rather than optimism. The Council should not assume optimism bias; it should require evidence one way or the other. But the Contrarian does not dispute that the *case as currently presented* lacks the discipline to support the stated confidence — only the conclusion that optimism bias is *the* explanation.

### 6.2 Public Value & Ethics

Ridership forecasts that overstate demand commit public capital to underutilised infrastructure. The duty of care to taxpayers warrants requiring demand-stress-testing before approval. Distributional concern: who benefits from the optimistic case being approved (sponsor, contractors), and who bears the cost if it underperforms (taxpayers, future operating subsidy)?

## 7. MAIN RECOMMENDATION

Do not approve on the case as presented. Require, before any commitment: (i) reference-class forecast of cost and demand alongside the bottom-up estimate, with P50/P80 ranges; (ii) outturn performance disclosure on the three prior projects (pre-FC estimate vs actual); (iii) BCR distribution under joint adverse movements (cost +30%, +50%; demand −20%, −30%; operating saving at zero); (iv) independent demand review. Confidence: high.

## 8. ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS

Approve as presented (rejected on optimism-bias and BCR-fragility grounds). Approve with conditions (primary recommendation, see §7). Defer pending strengthening (acceptable variant).
