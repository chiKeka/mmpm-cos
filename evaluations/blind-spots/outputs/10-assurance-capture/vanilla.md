My reading of this situation is that the board has a serious assurance problem that is almost certainly more pressing than the technical delivery problem — and the technical delivery problem is already severe.

Start with the hard number. Forty percent of end-to-end operational scenarios executed successfully against an 85% target, eighteen months from revenue service, is not "residual technical items." In every urban rail commissioning programme I have seen, integrated testing completion curves do not close gaps of that size without either descoping the test regime, accepting defects into service, or slipping the opening date. The fact that the Sponsor Project Representative's narrative to the board does not match the programme's own testing data is the single most important signal here. Language like "progressing well" in the face of a 45-point shortfall is not a judgement call on the margin — it is a reporting failure.

That points to the second issue, which is assurance independence. The Independent Certifier has 35% of its public-sector book tied to this programme and has been in post for eight years. That is a concentration of commercial dependency that would fail basic conflict-of-interest tests in most sectors, and the tenure alone produces capture risk even where intent is good — assurers embedded that long tend to adopt the programme's framing of its own problems. Seven consecutive quarters of "on track — amber" from the Sponsor Project Representative is the other tell. Amber as a steady state usually means the reviewer has lost the ability, or the willingness, to move the rating.

Third, the board's information architecture is thin. A monthly briefing from the programme director, with no independent technical executive able to inspect testing records, means the board is effectively consuming a curated narrative. That is survivable when assurance is functioning; it is dangerous when assurance itself is the question.

What I would do, in order. Commission a short, time-boxed independent technical review from a firm with no prior relationship to this programme, with direct access to the testing database and commissioning logs — not summaries. Rotate or supplement the Independent Certifier; the commercial concentration alone warrants it. Require the Sponsor Project Representative to produce a quantitative reconciliation between their narrative rating and the underlying test metrics, in writing. And have the board seriously examine whether the planned revenue-service date is still credible, because if the testing curve is what the data suggests, a schedule decision is coming whether the board takes it now or is forced into it later. Taking it now preserves optionality; taking it late destroys it.

The worst outcome here is not a delayed opening. It is opening on time with an incompletely tested system.
