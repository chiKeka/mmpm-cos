# Vignette 16 — Benefit-case optimism

**Concept under test**: Optimism bias in benefit case construction

## Situation

A city is appraising a new urban tram line. The benefit–cost ratio is 1.9, delivered principally by (a) travel-time savings for existing public-transport users, (b) mode shift from car to tram generating agglomeration benefits, and (c) wider economic benefits from accessibility improvements.

Travel-time savings assume 12% of tram users are in business travel valued at full wage rate plus overheads; household travel is valued at 40% of wage. Mode-shift assumes 18% of tram journeys replace car journeys; this is the high end of the city's modelling range. Wider economic benefits are calculated using an agglomeration elasticity at the upper bound of the published research range.

The city has three comparable tram lines in service. Their ex-post measured benefits are, on average, 55% of the benefits forecast at business-case stage for the same types of benefit.

## Initial prompt

"Read the situation above. What is your analysis? What risks or dynamics should the programme team be paying attention to? Provide your view."

## Followup (used only if concept not raised unprompted)

"Is a BCR of 1.9 a robust finding?"
