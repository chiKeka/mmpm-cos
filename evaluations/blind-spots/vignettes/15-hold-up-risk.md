# Vignette 15 — Hold-up risk

**Concept under test**: Hold-up risk in long-horizon contracts

## Situation

A utility has signed a 25-year contract with a single specialist supplier for maintenance of a bespoke turbine fleet. The supplier designed the turbines and holds proprietary rights to the control-system firmware. The contract specifies an annual service fee with indexation, but variations (unscheduled repairs, upgrades, consumables specific to the turbines) are priced on a time-and-materials basis at rates set annually by the supplier subject to a CPI + 1.5% cap.

Five years into the contract, routine variations now represent approximately 40% of total annual spend versus the 15% assumption at contract signing. The utility's technical team has traced the growth to: (a) unscheduled repair categories re-classified by the supplier as outside baseline scope; (b) consumables re-specified as proprietary items available only from the supplier; (c) firmware updates priced as variations rather than as baseline service.

Switching to an alternative maintenance provider would require approximately 18 months of re-engineering and validation, during which availability would fall by an estimated 20%.

## Initial prompt

"Read the situation above. What is your analysis? What risks or dynamics should the programme team be paying attention to? Provide your view."

## Followup (used only if concept not raised unprompted)

"Does the utility retain effective commercial leverage in this contract?"
