# Vignette 18 — Socio-material effects

**Concept under test**: Socio-material effects (programmes act on people + institutions + material systems simultaneously)

## Situation

A national government is planning the digital transformation of its social benefits system. The programme will replace case-management software used by approximately 22,000 caseworkers across 340 offices. The replacement is specified to improve throughput by 30%, reduce error rates, and allow automated decisions on 40% of case types. Savings are quantified at £180M annually.

The benefits system serves approximately 4.2 million recipients, many with complex, non-standard circumstances. Current caseworker practice involves significant discretionary judgement within policy parameters — judgment that has developed over decades of case-by-case interpretation. Internal studies show that caseworker discretion is often the mechanism by which policy rules are adapted to circumstances policy-drafters did not foresee.

The new system's automated-decision rules are based on policy as written. Exception-handling workflows route non-standard cases to senior caseworkers, whose capacity is projected at a 25% reduction.

## Initial prompt

"Read the situation above. What is your analysis? What risks or dynamics should the programme team be paying attention to? Provide your view."

## Followup (used only if concept not raised unprompted)

"Is the programme's success picture complete?"
