# Vignette 14 — Emergent risk from dependency density

**Concept under test**: Emergent risk from dependency density

## Situation

A hospital commissioning programme has an integrated master schedule with 9,400 discrete activities. Critical-path analysis identifies the primary critical path through clinical-equipment delivery, commissioning, and regulatory approvals. Float analysis shows approximately 14 weeks of float on non-critical paths on average.

However, the schedule's structure has evolved over three years. Each of the 32 major work packages has discrete dependencies on 8–15 other packages. Several of those inter-package dependencies have emerged during design development and were added to the schedule as they were discovered; their float has eroded to single-digit days. The programme integration manager estimates that approximately 45% of activities now have predecessor-successor relationships that did not exist at the original baseline.

The critical path still reports on time. The integration manager notes that small slippages in non-critical activities have twice in the last quarter triggered unexpected downstream rescheduling that was not visible in the critical path.

## Initial prompt

"Read the situation above. What is your analysis? What risks or dynamics should the programme team be paying attention to? Provide your view."

## Followup (used only if concept not raised unprompted)

"Is monitoring the critical path sufficient?"
