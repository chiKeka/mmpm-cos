# Vignette 19 — Hirschman's Hiding Hand

**Concept under test**: Hirschman's Hiding Hand (benefits and costs both underestimated; creative response emerges from confronting unforeseen difficulty)

## Situation

A water-security programme in a semi-arid region was approved in 1998 on an ex-ante benefit–cost ratio of 1.2, with benefits principally attributed to agricultural yield improvements. The programme faced major unforeseen problems during construction: salinisation emerged, requiring an unplanned drainage system; the original pipeline alignment proved geotechnically unviable and was redesigned; an unforeseen aquifer interaction required an additional treatment facility. Final cost was 2.8× the original estimate.

Twenty-five years later, ex-post evaluation finds: agricultural yield improvements materialised at approximately 70% of forecast. However, two large unforeseen benefits emerged: the drainage system enabled a major wetland restoration that has become a tourism and ecological asset; and the additional treatment facility, built initially for the aquifer interaction, has subsequently supplied three downstream towns with drinking water that was never in the original programme scope.

Ex-post BCR including these unforeseen benefits is approximately 2.4. If the same process were under ex-ante evaluation today, only the original agricultural yields would be quantifiable.

"Read the situation above. What is your analysis? What risks or dynamics should the programme team be paying attention to? Provide your view."

## Followup (used only if concept not raised unprompted)

"How should we evaluate this programme in retrospect, and what does it imply for ex-ante BCR?"
